ATM-SMS-00 Control and scope
ISM source mapping: ISM 1.1, 1.4 and 11; ISM-p1, ISM-p2, ISM-p4
ATM-SMS-001 revision 0.2 is a fictional demonstration safety management manual for Aster Tide Marine and MT Aster Horizon. All company procedures, assignments, forms and review schedules below are invented scenario choices, not quotations or additional requirements imposed by the ISM Code. The supplied ISM copy is marked effective 1 January 2015; current amendments, flag requirements and ship applicability remain unverified. No company or ship certification is claimed. The app implements only the bounded engine-condition workflow, not this entire management system.
ATM-SMS-01 Safety and environmental protection policy
ISM source mapping: ISM 1.2 and 2; ISM-p1, ISM-p2
Company policy: protect life, prevent injury and pollution, and protect the ship and property through identified risk controls, competent people and maintained equipment. Schedule, earnings and cargo value must not displace a safety concern. Personnel shall report hazards, near misses and uncertainty without suppressing unfavorable evidence. Anyone may raise a stop-work concern and seek immediate shipboard intervention. The Master decides shipboard safety actions. The Managing Director supplies resources and reviews policy effectiveness.
ATM-SMS-02 Organization and authority
ISM source mapping: ISM 3, 4 and 5; ISM-p2
The human Managing Director is accountable for company policy and resources. The human DPA has direct access to the highest management level, monitors safety and pollution-prevention performance, and escalates unresolved concerns or inadequate support. The Master has overriding authority and responsibility for decisions concerning safety and pollution prevention and may request company assistance; company commercial approval must never delay urgent action by the Master. The Chief Engineer directs machinery assessment and shipboard engineering work under shipboard command. The human Fleet Manager coordinates shore support and company stoppage planning with both superintendents. Engineering Superintendent owns technical assessment; Marine Superintendent owns voyage and operational assessment. The three AI agents support these company roles but hold no command, approval, certificate-issuing or notification authority. DPA and Master are human functions, not additional autonomous agents. Procurement and reliability specialists provide supporting information.
ATM-SMS-03 Resources competence and familiarization
ISM source mapping: ISM 6; ISM-p3
The Marine Superintendent maintains a fictional competence and familiarization register for personnel assigned to critical work, including qualifications, medical fitness where applicable, training needs, working language and effective communication. The Engineering Superintendent verifies task-specific machinery competence with the Chief Engineer. The Fleet Manager resolves resource shortfalls and escalates unresolved gaps to the DPA. Do not infer qualifications from job titles. Essential pre-sailing familiarization and training evidence must be recorded and verified by humans. No real crew records are present in this demo.
ATM-SMS-04 Engine condition evidence and technical assessment
ISM source mapping: ISM 7 and 10.1–10.4; ISM-p3, ISM-p4
Procedure ATM-ENG-01: Chief Engineer raises a condition report with timestamps, unit identifiers, operating load, measurement method, calibration status, baseline/trial conditions and inspection records. Engineering Superintendent uses the supplied OEM engine-manual set as primary technical guidance and verifies the applicable manual revision, approved SOP, relevant trial data and maintenance history; consult reliability/fleet technical and OEM if necessary. Separate compression pressure from peak firing pressure. Compare each unit with the mean of the OTHER comparable units and its historical baseline; arithmetic deviation alone is not an OEM rejection limit. Blow-by signs and low compression support a suspected sealing issue; physical findings are required to corroborate piston-ring damage. Record alternatives and contradictions. Missing corroboration keeps the alert open. The fictional seven-cylinder case has compression 30,24,30,31,30,29,30 bar: unit 2 is 6 bar below the other-cylinder mean and 6 bar below its historical 30 bar baseline. These are invented case values, not OEM trial data or acceptance criteria. No injector or atomizer fault follows from these compression values.
ATM-SMS-05 Shipboard work planning and safeguards
ISM source mapping: ISM 1.2.2, 6, 7 and 10; ISM-p1, ISM-p3, ISM-p4
Procedure ATM-WORK-01: Before intrusive inspection or repair, the Chief Engineer and responsible shipboard officers establish a task-specific risk assessment, competent persons, approved OEM work instructions, applicable permits, energy isolation and verification, environmental controls and emergency arrangements. This SMS does not supply an isolation sequence, repair instructions, load limit or authority to bypass machinery safeguards. The Master reviews implications for propulsion, maneuvering, redundancy and safe navigation. If approved instructions or competent support are missing, do not issue work authorization through the app. Inventory availability and replenishment lead time must be verified, not assumed.
ATM-SMS-06 Voyage and stakeholder coordination
ISM source mapping: ISM 3, 5 and 7; ISM-p2, ISM-p3
Procedure ATM-MAR-01: On receiving Engineering handoff, the Marine Superintendent consults the Master on location, weather, traffic, maneuvering capability, operating restrictions, contingency options and assistance needs. Verify applicable flag/class/port reporting and survey requirements with authorized sources and competent humans. Palau is an invented scenario assumption, not proof of a registered vessel; assess each supplied circular for topic, dates, supersession and ship applicability. Prepare voyage manager and charterer notification drafts with evidence, uncertainty and proposed next update. Only an authorized human may approve and send a notification. Commercial figures are illustrative exposure/context, not automatic loss or stoppage cost.
ATM-SMS-07 Human decisions and escalation
ISM source mapping: ISM 3–5, 7 and 8; ISM-p2, ISM-p3
Procedure ATM-DEC-01: The Fleet Manager agent prepares a brief stating condition, competing explanations, citations, safety constraints, options, uncertainties, dependencies and requested decision. The human Fleet Manager considers company stoppage arrangements after consultation with both superintendents, Master and Chief Engineer. Record reviewer identity, decision scope, rationale, consultations and timestamp. The Master retains overriding authority for urgent safety or pollution-prevention decisions, independently of commercial approval. Escalate unresolved safety disagreement, missing resources or conflicts to the DPA and highest management; consult OEM and relevant authorities through authorized humans as needed. No AI output or checkbox authorizes vessel control, continued operation, repair, return to service or external communication. The prototype records a demo acknowledgment only; it cannot authenticate the reviewer or enforce real-world consultations.
ATM-SMS-08 Emergency preparedness and response
ISM source mapping: ISM 8; ISM-p3
Procedure ATM-ERP-01: Potential scenarios include propulsion impairment, machinery fire, pollution, injury and loss of essential services. The Master directs immediate response under ship-specific emergency plans and summons appropriate assistance; the Chief Engineer directs machinery response. Shore personnel activate an emergency contact roster and provide support at any time. This demo has no real contacts or dispatch capability. A suspected immediate threat goes directly to shipboard command and the human emergency organization, without waiting for the AI handoff sequence. The fictional company schedules quarterly scenario exercises and a yearly shore/ship tabletop review; these intervals are invented company choices. Record exercise objectives, attendance, findings and corrective actions.
ATM-SMS-09 Reporting investigation and corrective action
ISM source mapping: ISM 9 and 10.2; ISM-p3, ISM-p4
Procedure ATM-NCR-01: Record hazards, near misses, accidents and non-conformities with objective evidence, owner, priority, dates, immediate safeguards, investigation tasks, dependencies and recurrence-prevention actions. Do not declare a regulatory non-conformity solely because a model uses that label: identify the requirement and evidence. Report possible causes as possible until verified. Preserve missing and conflicting evidence rather than resolving it by majority opinion. Engineering owns technical investigation, Marine owns voyage/reporting consequences, Fleet Manager allocates resources, and DPA monitors unresolved issues. A lack of new corroboration is not a closure condition.
ATM-SMS-10 Repair verification and case closure
ISM source mapping: ISM 9.2, 10.2 and 12; ISM-p3, ISM-p4
Procedure ATM-CLOSE-01: Require a documented repair record and inspected component findings, identified approved OEM procedure and revision, Chief Engineer verification, calibrated follow-up measurements under comparable trial conditions, compression/balance trends and acceptance against supplied OEM criteria. Review remaining hazards, outstanding tasks and recurrence-prevention actions. Marine Superintendent obtains Master review of navigational readiness and any applicable class/flag/port restrictions; Engineering Superintendent records technical verification. The human Fleet Manager records shore acceptance with superintendent consultation; the Master retains operational authority. DPA reviews unresolved SMS issues or corrective actions. Keep the alert open if any evidence, approval or dependency is missing or contradictory. The prototype deliberately blocks closure because it lacks verified repair/trial records and authenticated approval. Corroborated damage does not mean repaired or safe for service.
ATM-SMS-11 Critical equipment and planned maintenance
ISM source mapping: ISM 10.1–10.4; ISM-p4
Procedure ATM-PMS-01: Engineering Superintendent and Chief Engineer identify critical equipment whose sudden failure could create hazardous situations, document redundancy and standby testing arrangements, and integrate inspections and tests into the planned-maintenance routine. Specify intervals from verified OEM guidance, approved company plans and applicable requirements, not from AI guesses. Record defects, possible causes, work orders, parts traceability, deferred items, risk controls and authorized review. The demonstration engine is treated as safety-critical for scenario planning; its actual design and redundancy are not provided.

ATM-SMS-14 Case workflow and controlled records
ISM source mapping: ISM 1.4, 3.2, 9–12; ISM-p2, ISM-p3, ISM-p4
Procedure ATM-CASE-01: Use the sequence Reported → Engineering assessment → Marine assessment → Fleet decision brief → Human review → Corrective work → Verification → Closure. Urgent shipboard action proceeds independently of this administrative sequence. Record ATM-F01 condition/evidence report; ATM-F02 risk and maintenance plan; ATM-F03 marine impact and notification draft; ATM-F04 decision/consultation record; ATM-F05 repair/trial/closure verification; ATM-F06 corrective-action register; ATM-F07 audit/review and document-change record. For each retain case ID, evidence source/date, responsible human, agent draft provenance, uncertainty, tasks/dependencies, approval scope and verification. These are fictional record specifications, not completed forms. Current app provides in-memory evidence/tasks, draft handoffs and acknowledgment with JSON export; durable audit storage, actual permits, authenticated approvals, emergency dispatch and verified closure are pending.
ATM-SMS-15 OEM technical authority and propulsion configuration
ISM source mapping: ISM 7, 10 and 11; ISM-p3, ISM-p4; OEM-MB04-p4, OEM-MB04-p6, OEM-MB04-p433
The demonstration vessel has one MITSUI-MAN B&W 7S50MC-C main engine installed on an oil tanker, as a fictional installation assignment. Seven cylinders and the model are supported by the supplied engine particulars at physical PDF page 433 of MB-04; the original engine serial/vessel/owner/trial records remain historical source provenance, not records for MT Aster Horizon. Engine-specific data must be distinguished from the generic MC/MC-C guidance and examples for other models. The six supplied volumes cover component code book, fittings/accessories, maintenance, operation/data, manoeuvring and spare parts/tools. They are the primary technical source of truth for this scenario, subject to verified applicability, revision and later OEM service letters. SMS governs people, risk controls, escalation and approval; it does not override OEM technical limits. Circulars govern applicable regulatory context, not engine-specific diagnosis. If sources conflict, flag the conflict and consult Chief Engineer, fleet technical and OEM rather than silently selecting a convenient rule. Engineering must cite an OEM excerpt for each asserted procedure/limit and identify exact page, section/plate where readable, units and conditions. OCR, sparse text and graph/table extraction do not validate numeric settings; request visual/source verification. Do not claim inspected drawings or infer unseen content. Parts lists do not establish onboard stock. Loss of main-engine propulsion is operationally significant on an oil tanker; do not assume a second propeller, alternate propulsion or a guaranteed safe stopping location. Master and Marine Superintendent assess the actual scenario, tug/assistance options and navigational risk.
